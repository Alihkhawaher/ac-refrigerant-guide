## 1. When Flushing Is Required

Flushing refrigerant lines is a critical maintenance procedure in specific scenarios. The following table summarizes when flushing is mandatory, recommended, or unnecessary.

| Scenario | Flush Required? | Reason |
|----------|:---:|---------|
| **Compressor burnout** (motor/mechanical failure) | ✅ YES — Always | Acid, carbon sludge, and metal particles contaminate the entire line set |
| **R-22 to R-410A retrofit** | ✅ YES — Always | Mineral oil from R-22 is incompatible with POE oil used in R-410A |
| **R-22 to R-32 / R-454B (A2L) retrofit** | ✅ YES — Always | Old mineral/alkylbenzene oil incompatible with new A2L refrigerant oils |
| **Compressor replacement** (preventive, still running) | ⚠️ Recommended | Preventive measure to remove any accumulated debris |
| **Refrigerant leak repair** (no compressor change) | ❌ No | System remains sealed; no contamination introduced |
| **Filter drier replacement** | ❌ No | Drier captures contaminants; no flush needed |
| **New installation with new line set** | ❌ No | New copper lines are clean from factory |

> **Key Principle:** If the system has been opened to atmosphere, or if a burnout has occurred, flushing is mandatory. The only exception is when replacing the entire line set with new copper tubing.

**Sources:** [HCPRO Burnout Flush Guide](https://www.hcproproducts.com/blogs/news/how-to-flush-hvac-after-compressor-burnout), [ACHR News Guidelines](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/85492-guidelines-for-flushing-contaminated-systems), [AD Engineering](https://ad.engineering/how-to-flush-hvac-line-sets-before-system-recharge/)

## 2. Understanding Contaminants

### 2.1 What a Compressor Burnout Leaves Behind

When a hermetic compressor burns out, the high-temperature electrical arcing breaks down the refrigerant oil and refrigerant, creating three categories of contamination:

| Contaminant | Description | Effect on New Compressor |
|-------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Acid** (hydrochloric HCl, hydrofluoric HF) | Formed when refrigerant oil breaks down at high temperature. Chlorine and fluorine from refrigerant combine with moisture | Eats copper, brass, compressor windings. Dissolves motor insulation. Creates conductive paths |
| **Carbon sludge** | Flakes from burned compressor windings and thermally degraded oil | Lodges in TXV, capillary tubes, EEV. Circulates into new compressor causing mechanical damage |
| **Metal particles** | Debris from failed compressor bearings, windings, and internal components | Abrasive damage to new compressor. Clogs metering devices |
| **Decomposed oil** (varnish/tar) | Mineral or POE oil thermally degraded into sticky residue | Restricts oil flow. Coats internal surfaces. Prevents proper lubrication |

> **Critical:** Skip the flush and these contaminants push into the new compressor on first start. The result is **repeat burnout within 60–90 days**.

**Source:** [HCPRO Burnout Flush Guide](https://www.hcproproducts.com/blogs/news/how-to-flush-hvac-after-compressor-burnout)

### 2.2 What a Refrigerant Conversion Leaves Behind

When converting from R-22 (mineral oil) to R-410A or A2L refrigerants (POE oil):

| Contaminant | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Mineral oil residue** | Coats copper walls. Incompatible with POE oil. Creates sludge when mixed |
| **Alkylbenzene oil** (if present) | Similar incompatibility with POE |
| **Moisture** | Mineral oil systems may have absorbed moisture over years of operation |

**Source:** [AD Engineering](https://ad.engineering/how-to-flush-hvac-line-sets-before-system-recharge/)

### 2.3 Assessing Burnout Severity

Before flushing, determine the severity of contamination:

| Test | Method | Result |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Oil acid test** | Extract oil sample from compressor sump. Use acid test kit (e.g., Nu-Calgon Phase III) | Green = no acid (mild burnout). Yellow/brown = acid present (severe burnout) |
| **Visual inspection** | Check suction line interior for carbon deposits | Black soot = severe contamination |
| **Refrigerant smell** | Smell of burned refrigerant | Pungent, acrid odor = severe burnout |

> **Rule:** If in doubt, treat as severe burnout. Even a minor burnout leaves acid that will eventually destroy the replacement compressor.

**Source:** [ACHR News - Performing a Clean-up After a Burnout](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/102564-performing-a-clean-up-after-a-burnout)

## 3. Material Compatibility: Copper vs Aluminum

### 3.1 Copper Refrigerant Lines

Copper is the standard material for refrigerant lines in residential and commercial HVAC systems.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Chemical resistance** | Copper is **immune** to all common flushing solvents — thinner, benzine, alcohol, brake cleaner, RX-11, Pro-Flush, etc. |
| **Corrosion risk** | None from solvents. Copper does not react with hydrocarbon solvents, alcohols, or HFC-based flush agents |
| **Temperature tolerance** | Handles all flushing temperatures without degradation |
| **Conclusion** | **All flushing agents are safe for copper lines.** The choice of solvent should be based on cleaning effectiveness, not material compatibility |

### 3.2 Aluminum Components (Evaporator/Condenser Coils)

Aluminum is used in evaporator and condenser coil fins and, in some systems, in the coil tubing itself (microchannel coils).

| Condition | Solvent Effect | Risk Level |
|-----------|---------------|:---:|
| **Bare aluminum** (uncoated) | Brief exposure to solvents is safe. No aggressive reaction | ✅ Low |
| **Prolonged exposure** (hours of soaking) | Can cause mild oxidation/surface corrosion | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Hydrophilic fin coating** (blue/gold) | Solvents can strip or degrade the coating over time | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Microchannel coils** (thin aluminum walls) | More sensitive due to thinner material. Acid from burnout can pit these coils | ❌ High |

> **Important:** During a line flush, the solvent flows **inside the copper tubing only**. The aluminum fins are on the **outside** of the tubes and are not exposed to the flushing solvent. The only scenario where solvent contacts aluminum is when flushing **through** the coil itself (copper tubes inside the coil), where the solvent only touches the copper interior.

### 3.3 Components Exposed to Flush Solvent

The solvent flows through the interior of the refrigerant circuit. The following components are exposed:

- **Copper tube interior walls** — immune to all solvents
- **Schrader valve cores** — **removed before flushing** (not because solvent damages them, but because removal improves flow and vacuum speed). Replace after burnout as cheap insurance
- **Service valve O-rings** — compatible with professional flush solvents (RX-11, Pro-Flush, Purge are formulated to be safe with rubber). No removal needed
- **TXV/expansion valve internals** — **must be bypassed or removed before flushing** (small passages clog easily)
- **Filter drier internals** — **must be removed before flushing** (saturated with contaminants)
- **Capillary tube / fixed orifice** — **must be bypassed or removed before flushing** (small passages clog easily)
- **Check valve internals** — **must be bypassed or removed before flushing** (can trap contaminants)

> **Note on rubber compatibility:** Professional HVAC flush solvents (RX-11, Pro-Flush, Purge) are specifically formulated to be compatible with all system materials including rubber seals and O-rings. Brief contact during a flush does not damage rubber. Budget solvents (thinner, IPA) are more aggressive but the short contact time during flush followed by immediate N₂ purge makes damage unlikely.

## 6. Components to Isolate or Replace

### 6.1 Components to REMOVE from Flush Path

These components must be **removed or bypassed** before flushing. The solvent should only flow through copper tubing.

| Component | Action | Reason |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **Compressor** | Remove entirely | Solvent would contaminate new compressor oil. Flush only the line set |
| **Filter drier (liquid line)** | Remove. Install new one after flush | Saturated with acid and contaminants. Cannot be cleaned |
| **Filter drier (suction line)** | Remove. Install new one after flush | Same as above |
| **TXV / Expansion valve** | Remove or bypass with loop | Internal passages are small. Carbon can clog them. Diaphragm can be damaged by solvent |
| **Capillary tube / Fixed orifice** | Remove or bypass | Same as TXV — small passages clog easily |
| **Check valves** | Remove or bypass | Internal mechanisms can trap contaminants |
| **Four-way reversing valve** (heat pumps) | Remove or bypass | Complex internal passages trap solvent and contaminants |
| **Schrader valve cores** | Remove | Improves flow and vacuum speed. Replace after burnout as cheap insurance |
| **Sight glass / moisture indicator** | Remove or bypass | Can be contaminated |
| **Mufflers** (if present) | Remove or bypass | Internal baffles trap contaminants |

### 6.2 Components to REPLACE (Mandatory After Burnout)

| Component | Why Replace |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Filter driers** (both suction and liquid line) | Saturated with acid. Cannot be regenerated. Must be replaced every time |
| **Schrader valve cores** | Burned cores can leak. Cheap insurance |
| **Compressor oil** | Old oil is acidic. Must be replaced with fresh POE oil |
| **Vacuum pump oil** | Contaminated pump oil won't pull deep vacuum |

### 6.3 Components to INSPECT

| Component | What to Check |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Indoor coil (evaporator)** | If severely contaminated, may need replacement. Mildly contaminated coils can be cleaned with manufacturer-approved coil cleaners after line flush |
| **Outdoor coil (condenser)** | Same as evaporator. Usually less contaminated since it's on the high-pressure side |
| **Line set condition** | Check for kinks, corrosion, damage. If accessible and damaged, replacement is preferred over flushing |
| **Electrical connections** | Check for burned wires, contactor damage |

> **Note on residential systems:** In many regions, residential AC systems use capillary tubes or fixed orifice (piston) metering devices rather than TXV. These should still be removed or bypassed during flushing due to their small internal passages.

**Sources:** [ACHR News Guidelines](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/85492-guidelines-for-flushing-contaminated-systems), [Nu-Calgon RX-11 Instructions](https://www.nucalgon.com/media/6814/3-112_rx11-flush.pdf), [HVAC School Burnout Class](http://www.hvacrschool.com/videos/system-burnout-cleanup-procedures-class/)

## 7. Step-by-Step Flush Procedure

### Stage 1: Recovery and Isolation (15–30 minutes)

**Step 1: Recover all refrigerant**
- Connect recovery machine to service ports
- Recover per EPA 608 protocol
- Document refrigerant type and amount recovered
- Reclaim or destroy — **never vent to atmosphere**

> **Reference:** [HCPRO Stage 1](https://www.hcproproducts.com/blogs/news/how-to-flush-hvac-after-compressor-burnout) — "Recover all refrigerant per EPA 608 protocol. Use a recovery machine rated for the refrigerant type."

**Step 2: Remove the burned compressor**
- Disconnect electrical leads
- Braze off suction and discharge lines
- Remove compressor from system
- Drain and document oil condition (for acid test)

**Step 3: Isolate indoor and outdoor coils**
- Disconnect line set from both coil connections
- Cap all coil openings to prevent contamination
- The solvent will flush through the **line set only**, not through the coils

**Step 4: Remove all inline components**
- Remove filter driers (install bypass loops if needed)
- Remove TXV, capillary tube, or fixed orifice
- Remove check valves and reversing valve (heat pumps)
- Remove Schrader valve cores from service ports
- Remove sight glass / moisture indicator

> **Reference:** [ACHR News Step 4](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/85492-guidelines-for-flushing-contaminated-systems) — "Remove filter driers. If it is easier or more cost-effective, install a bypass loop around the filter drier accumulators."

**Step 5: Section long line sets (if needed)**
- For runs longer than 15 m (50 ft), cut into sections
- Flush each section separately for better pressure distribution
- Mark sections for reassembly
- Plan to braze sections back together after flushing

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #3](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "Lengths of 50, 75, 100-feet or longer should be cut, flushed in sections, and then soldered back together."

### Stage 2: Nitrogen Pre-Purge (5–10 minutes)

**Step 6: Push dry nitrogen through lines**
- Connect nitrogen regulator to one end of line set
- Set pressure to **120–150 PSI (8–10 bar)**
- Blow nitrogen through to dislodge loose debris
- Oscillate the purge (pulse on/off) to help dislodge stuck contaminants
- This step removes loose particles BEFORE introducing solvent

> **Why nitrogen, not shop air?** Compressed shop air contains moisture and oil from the compressor. Moisture + refrigerant = acid. Always use dry nitrogen.

> **Reference:** [AD Engineering Step 1](https://ad.engineering/how-to-flush-hvac-line-sets-before-system-recharge/) — "Push nitrogen through the lines at approximately 120 psi to dislodge loose debris before introducing any solvent. This is a critical first step."

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #1](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "Purging loose debris first with nitrogen allows the flushing agent to attack contaminants that adhere to the inner walls of the linesets."

### Stage 3: Solvent Flush (15–30 minutes)

**Step 7: Connect flush equipment**
- Connect flush canister / injection tool to one end of line set
- Connect collection container to the other end
- **Restrict (crimp) the exit** to increase contact time and pressure inside the line
- Solder a ¼" flare fitting for best pressure connection (preferred over rubber adapters)

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #5](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "Crimping the opposite end of the lineset will induce more pressure within the lineset and help dislodge additional contaminants."

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #7](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "The best method to get maximum pressure is soldering a ¼-inch flare fitting onto the end of the lineset."

**Step 8: Inject solvent**
- Open valve on injection tool to introduce solvent
- Apply nitrogen at **120–150 PSI (8–10 bar)** behind the solvent
- For aerosol cans: use 20–90 second bursts per 15 m (50 ft) of line (depending on diameter)
- For liquid solvents: pour into canister, pressurize with N₂, inject

**Solvent quantity guide:**

| System Size | RX-11 | Pro-Flush | Purge | Flash Flush |
|-------------|-------|-----------|-------|-------------|
| 1–3 tons | 1 lb can | 8 oz | 1 lb | 16 oz |
| 3–5 tons | 1–2 lb | 8–16 oz | 1 lb | 16–32 oz |
| 5–7 tons | 2 lb | 16 oz | 1–2 lb | 32 oz |
| 7–10 tons | 2 lb | 16 oz | 2 lb | 32 oz |
| 10–15 tons | 2+ lb | 16+ oz | 2 lb | 32 oz |

> **Reference:** [Nu-Calgon RX-11 Instructions](https://www.nucalgon.com/media/6814/3-112_rx11-flush.pdf) — "1 lb. canister will flush 3-4 ton systems, 2 lb. canister will flush 5-7 ton systems."

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #2](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "A good rule of thumb is one 2-lb. canister of flush for a 7-to-10-ton refrigeration system."

**Step 9: Flush direction**
- Always flush **from inside to outside** (indoor unit to outdoor) to avoid contaminating living spaces
- If indoor is lower than outdoor, flush outside to inside but cover the area with tarps
- Flush from high side to low side when possible

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #5](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "Always flush from inside of the mechanical room to outdoors to eliminate contaminating living spaces."

**Step 10: Inspect runoff**
- Watch the solvent exiting into the collection container
- **Dark/cloudy with particles** = still contaminated → continue flushing
- **Clear and clean** = flush complete
- Near the end, switch to a clean container to verify clarity
- For severe burnouts, repeat with additional solvent until runoff is crystal clear

> **Reference:** [ReliabilityWeb Tip #9](https://reliabilityweb.com/news/article/hvac_service_tips_on_flushing_linesets_and_burnouts) — "Don't stop flushing until the terminating liquid in the bucket becomes clear and particulate free."

**Step 11: Final nitrogen purge**
- Immediately after solvent flush, connect nitrogen to the line
- Push nitrogen through at **150 PSI (10 bar) for 1–2 minutes**
- This removes all residual solvent and suspended contaminants
- If exiting gas is still cloudy, repeat the purge
- Wait 5 minutes for any remaining solvent to flash off, then purge again

> **Reference:** [HCPRO Stage 2 Step 6](https://www.hcproproducts.com/blogs/news/how-to-flush-hvac-after-compressor-burnout) — "Blow out with dry nitrogen for 30–60 seconds to ensure no solvent remains."

> **Reference:** [ACHR News Step 8](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/85492-guidelines-for-flushing-contaminated-systems) — "Purge the system at 150 psi for one to two minutes to remove all trace amounts of oil residue and solvents."

### Stage 4: Reassembly (30–60 minutes)

**Step 12: Reconnect sectioned lines** (if cut)
- Braze sections back together
- **Flow nitrogen during brazing** to prevent oxidation scale inside tubes
- Use braze-setting on nitrogen regulator (low flow)

> **Reference:** [All Temp Solutions](https://www.alltempsolutionsinc.com/post/replacing-compressor-on-ac-unit) — "Always use a low nitrogen sweep during braze" to prevent "black flakes/scale inside lines."

**Step 13: Install new compressor**
- Follow manufacturer's installation instructions
- Use new mounting grommets if specified
- Braze connections with nitrogen flow

**Step 14: Install new components**
- New filter driers (suction line + liquid line)
- New Schrader valve cores
- TXV / metering device (if removed)
- Check valves / reversing valve (if removed)
- Sight glass / moisture indicator (recommended)

**Step 15: Pressure test**
- Pressurize system with dry nitrogen to **150–300 PSI (10–20 bar)**
- Hold for **10 minutes**
- Check for pressure drop — no drop = no leaks
- Bubble-test all joints with soapy water if any doubt

> **Reference:** [All Temp Solutions](https://www.alltempsolutionsinc.com/post/replacing-compressor-on-ac-unit) — "Introduce dry nitrogen to ~150 psi and let it stabilize. Hold 10–15 minutes; watch for pressure drop."

### Stage 5: Vacuum and Evacuation (60–90 minutes)

See [Section 8: Vacuum and Evacuation Procedure](#8-vacuum-and-evacuation-procedure) for detailed steps.

### Stage 6: Recharge and Commission (30–60 minutes)

**Step 22: Add compressor oil**
- Drain oil from new compressor (if pre-charged)
- Fill with fresh POE oil per compressor manufacturer spec
- Typical residential R-410A scroll: 700–1100 mL (25–40 oz)
- Mini-split compressors: 220–500 mL (8–18 oz)

> **Reference:** [HCPRO Stage 4 Step 4](https://www.hcproproducts.com/blogs/news/how-to-flush-hvac-after-compressor-burnout) — "Match the new compressor's OEM spec. Use RPO-32 POE for R-410A, R-32, R-134a, R-1234yf, R-404A, and A2L systems."

**Step 23: Recharge with refrigerant**
- Weigh in refrigerant per equipment nameplate
- Account for line set length beyond nameplate training charge
- Start with recovered amount (if reusing), then adjust to spec

**Step 24: Start up and test**
- Run system for 15 minutes to stabilize
- Check pressures, superheat, and subcooling
- Document all readings
- Adjust charge if needed (TXV: target subcooling. Fixed orifice: target superheat)

> **Reference:** [All Temp Solutions](https://www.alltempsolutionsinc.com/post/replacing-compressor-on-ac-unit) — "Let the system run 15 minutes before making big adjustments."

**Step 25: Post-startup monitoring**
- Monitor suction line filter drier pressure drop after 1 hour
- If pressure drop is excessive → replace driers
- Test oil acidity after 24 hours
- If oil is still acidic → replace driers again
- Recheck oil at 2 weeks to confirm system is stable

> **Reference:** [ACHR News - Performing a Clean-up After a Burnout](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/102564-performing-a-clean-up-after-a-burnout) — "In 24 hours take a lubricant sample. Observe the color and test for acidity. If the lubricant is dirty or acidic, replace the suction line and liquid line filter-driers. In two weeks re-check."

## 8. Vacuum and Evacuation Procedure

Proper evacuation is critical after any flush. The vacuum removes residual solvent AND moisture from the system.

### 8.1 Equipment Setup

| Requirement | Specification |
|-------------|---------------|
| **Vacuum pump** | Minimum 1.4 L/s (3 CFM) residential. 2.8+ L/s (6+ CFM) commercial. Two-stage preferred |
| **Vacuum hoses** | 3/8" or 1/2" diameter. **Never use 1/4" hoses** — they restrict flow by 16x. Metric equiv: 10 mm or 13 mm (not 6 mm) |
| **Micron gauge** | Electronic, reads in microns. Connect as far from pump as possible |
| **Core removal tools** | Remove Schrader cores for full-flow evacuation |
| **Pump oil** | Fresh ISO VG46. Change before every burnout recovery |

> **Reference:** [TruTech Tools](https://trutechtools.com/air-conditioning-system-evacuation-training.html) — "1/2" hoses will decrease the time required for evacuation by a factor of 16 times over the typical 1/4" hoses."

> **Reference:** [HVAC School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsnQeUSuUMU) — "Connect the hoses back to the vacuum pump and attach your micron gauge to the system (as far away from the pump as possible)."

### 8.2 Evacuation Steps

**Step A: Connect vacuum setup**
- Remove Schrader cores from both service ports
- Connect 3/8" or 1/2" (10 mm or 13 mm) vacuum hoses to core removal tools
- Connect hoses to vacuum pump via brass flare tee (eliminate manifold if possible)
- Connect micron gauge to system side (not pump side)

**Step B: Initial pull**
- Start vacuum pump
- Open gas ballast if system may be wet
- Watch micron gauge drop from 760,000 microns (atmospheric)
- When gauge reaches ~3,000 microns, close gas ballast

> **Reference:** [All Temp Solutions](https://www.alltempsolutionsinc.com/post/replacing-compressor-on-ac-unit) — "Once the micron gauge gets around 3,000 microns, I close the gas ballast."

**Step C: First standing test (at 1,000 microns)**
- Continue vacuum to 1,000 microns
- Isolate pump by closing core tools
- Wait 5 minutes for stabilization
- Record leak rate

> **Reference:** [TruTech Tools](https://trutechtools.com/air-conditioning-system-evacuation-training.html) — "Pull a vacuum until a level of 1000 microns is reached. Isolate the vacuum with the core tools allowing the pump to continue to run and record the leak rate."

**Step D: Second standing test (at 500 microns)**
- Open core tools, continue to 500 microns or below
- Isolate pump again
- Wait 10–15 minutes
- **Reading must not rise above 1,000 microns** (ideally stays below 500)
- Any significant rise = leak or moisture → investigate

> **Reference:** [AC Units Guide](https://www.acunitsguide.com/how-to-vacuum-ac-system) — "The reading should not rise above 1,000-1,500 microns. If it rises quickly, you have a leak or remaining moisture."

> **Reference:** [HVAC School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsnQeUSuUMU) — "Pulling below 500 microns, isolating for 10 minutes, and making sure the pressure doesn't rise above 1000 microns will suffice."

**Step E: Final vacuum**
- Continue pumping to below 200 microns (ideally 50–100)
- Isolate system
- Stand for 15–30 minutes
- If micron level stays below 500 → evacuation complete

> **Reference:** [TruTech Tools](https://trutechtools.com/air-conditioning-system-evacuation-training.html) — "After the second standing test, allow the vacuum pump to run until the system is preferably below 200 microns. Isolate the vacuum rig and allow the system to stand for 15 to 30 minutes. If the micron level does not rise above 500 microns the evacuation is complete."

### 8.3 Vacuum Depth Reference

| Micron Reading | Status | Action |
|:---:|---------|---------|
| Below 200 | Excellent | System is very dry and tight |
| 200–500 | Good | Acceptable. Proceed to recharge |
| 500–1,000 | Acceptable with decay test | Perform 10-min decay test. If holds under 1,000 → OK |
| 1,000–2,000 | Marginal | Likely residual moisture. Continue vacuum or change pump oil |
| Above 2,000 | Unacceptable | Check for leaks. Do not release refrigerant |

**Sources:** [AC Units Guide](https://www.acunitsguide.com/how-to-vacuum-ac-system), [TruTech Tools](https://trutechtools.com/air-conditioning-system-evacuation-training.html), [HVAC School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsnQeUSuUMU)

### 8.4 Triple Evacuation (for Wet Systems)

If moisture is suspected, use the nitrogen sweep method:

1. Pull vacuum to 1,000–2,500 microns
2. Isolate pump, disconnect vacuum hose from low side
3. Break vacuum with **dry nitrogen** to atmospheric pressure (760,000 microns)
4. Purge nitrogen through system at 1–3 PSIG (0.07–0.2 bar) from high to low side
5. Reconnect vacuum, pull down again
6. Repeat up to 3 times (triple evacuation)
7. Each cycle should reach deeper vacuum faster

> **Reference:** [TruTech Tools](https://trutechtools.com/air-conditioning-system-evacuation-training.html) — "If the system indicates moisture, a multiple evacuation with a nitrogen sweep will significantly reduce the amount of moisture in the system."

## 9. Post-Flush Verification

After the system is running, verify that the flush was successful:

| Check | When | Pass Criteria |
|-------|------|---------------|
| **Suction line drier pressure drop** | 1 hour after startup | Below manufacturer's maximum (typically <5 PSI / 0.35 bar) |
| **Oil acid test** | 24 hours after startup | No acid detected (green on test kit) |
| **Oil color** | 24 hours | Clear, not dark or cloudy |
| **Oil acid test** | 2 weeks after startup | Still no acid |
| **System pressures** | At startup and 24h | Normal superheat/subcooling. Typical R-410A: suction 45–70 PSI (3–5 bar), discharge 220–360 PSI (15–25 bar) |
| **Compressor amp draw** | At startup | Within nameplate range (typical residential: 8–15 A at 220–240V) |

> **If oil is still acidic at 24 hours:** Replace both filter driers, retest at 24 hours again. Repeat until oil is clean.

> **Reference:** [ACHR News - Burned Up Over Burnouts](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/104537-burned-up-over-burnouts) — "If acid is treated or the system is replaced, it's a good practice to perform frequent service calls until future acid tests prove negative."

## 10. Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

| # | Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|:---:|---------|-------------|------------|
| 1 | **Skipping the flush** ("it was only a small burnout") | Repeat burnout in 60–90 days | Always flush. Even minor burnouts leave acid |
| 2 | **Using shop air instead of nitrogen** | Introduces moisture → acid formation | Always use dry nitrogen |
| 3 | **Not replacing filter driers** | Old driers are saturated with contamination | Replace both suction and liquid line driers every time |
| 4 | **Not changing vacuum pump oil** | Contaminated oil won't pull below 500 microns | Use fresh ISO VG46 oil before every evacuation |
| 5 | **Using 1/4" vacuum hoses** | 16x slower evacuation. May never reach target | Use 3/8" or 1/2" hoses (10 mm or 13 mm) |
| 6 | **Leaving Schrader cores in during vacuum** | Restricts flow, slow evacuation | Remove cores with core tools |
| 7 | **Pulling vacuum without micron gauge** | Guessing. Can't verify system is dry | Always use electronic micron gauge |
| 8 | **Hold time too short** | Moisture not fully removed | Minimum 15 minutes at 500 microns (0.5 mbar) |
| 9 | **Using wrong oil type** | Mineral oil in R-410A system = failure | R-410A requires POE oil. Check compressor spec |
| 10 | **Undersizing the flush solvent** | Incomplete cleaning. Contaminants remain | Use full recommended amount. Have extra on hand |
| 11 | **Not restricting exit during flush** | Solvent passes too quickly, poor cleaning | Crimp exit or use smaller opening |
| 12 | **Stopping flush before runoff is clear** | Residual contamination | Keep flushing until crystal clear |
| 13 | **Not flowing nitrogen during braze** | Oxidation scale inside tubes | Always use nitrogen sweep while brazing |
| 14 | **Releasing refrigerant before decay test** | System may still be wet | Always perform decay test first |
| 15 | **Not documenting the flush** | Warranty claims denied | Keep flush invoice, N₂ pressure test, vacuum readings |

**Sources:** [HCPRO](https://www.hcproproducts.com/blogs/news/how-to-flush-hvac-after-compressor-burnout), [All Temp Solutions](https://www.alltempsolutionsinc.com/post/replacing-compressor-on-ac-unit), [AC Units Guide](https://www.acunitsguide.com/how-to-vacuum-ac-system)

## 11. Safety Precautions

| Hazard | Precaution |
|--------|------------|
| **Solvent exposure** | Wear rubber gloves and safety glasses. All flush solvents have some degree of toxicity |
| **Flammable solvents** | No open flames, no smoking, no sparks. Ensure ventilation. HCPRO Flash Flush is flammable — keep away from brazing operations until fully purged |
| **Acid contact** | Burnout sludge contains acid. Wear rubber gloves. Avoid skin contact. Acid burns are possible |
| **High pressure nitrogen** | Never exceed system working pressure. Use regulated nitrogen at 120–150 PSI (8–10 bar) max for flushing |
| **Pressurized canisters** | Never connect flush canister directly to nitrogen source. Use injection valve with pressure relief. Canister may burst if over-pressurized |
| **Solvent splash** | Convoluted piping can cause erratic solvent flow. Protect eyes and skin |
| **Ventilation** | Work in well-ventilated area. Solvent vapors can cause dizziness |
| **Disposal** | Used solvent contains oil, acid, and contaminants. Dispose per local hazardous waste regulations. Do not pour down drains |
| **Refrigerant handling** | EPA Section 608 certification required (US) or local equivalent. Never vent refrigerant to atmosphere |

> **Reference:** [Nu-Calgon RX-11 Instructions](https://www.nucalgon.com/media/6814/3-112_rx11-flush.pdf) — "Safety equipment: Never flush a system without adequate face protection and rubber gloves."

> **Reference:** [ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/85492-guidelines-for-flushing-contaminated-systems) — "Do not smoke or use an open flame around these materials."

## Appendix A: Quick Reference — Burnout Recovery Checklist

```
COMPRESSION BURNOUT RECOVERY CHECKLIST
=======================================

□ 1. Recover all refrigerant (EPA 608)
□ 2. Remove burned compressor
□ 3. Isolate indoor and outdoor coils
□ 4. Remove all inline components (driers, TXV, check valves, Schrader cores)
□ 5. Section long line sets (>15 m / 50 ft)
□ 6. N₂ pre-purge at 120-150 PSI (8-10 bar)
□ 7. Connect flush canister + collection container
□ 8. Restrict exit for pressure buildup
□ 9. Inject solvent + N₂ at 120-150 PSI (8-10 bar)
□ 10. Flush until runoff is CLEAR
□ 11. N₂ final purge at 150 PSI (10 bar) for 1-2 minutes
□ 12. Reconnect sectioned lines (with N₂ braze sweep)
□ 13. Install new compressor
□ 14. Install new filter driers (suction + liquid line)
□ 15. Install new Schrader cores
□ 16. Reinstall TXV / metering device
□ 17. Pressure test at 150-300 PSI (10-20 bar) for 10 min
□ 18. Connect vacuum pump (3/8" or 1/2" / 10 mm or 13 mm hoses, cores removed)
□ 19. Pull vacuum to 500 microns (0.5 mbar)
□ 20. Decay test: hold 10-15 min, verify <1000 micron (1 mbar) rise
□ 21. Add fresh POE oil per compressor spec
□ 22. Recharge with refrigerant (weigh per nameplate)
□ 23. Start system, run 15 min
□ 24. Check pressures, superheat, subcooling
□ 25. Check suction drier pressure drop at 1 hour
□ 26. Oil acid test at 24 hours
□ 27. Oil acid test at 2 weeks
□ 28. Document everything for warranty file
```
